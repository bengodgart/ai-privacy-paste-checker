Sensitive data is now about a third of what employees paste into ChatGPT. Up from about 1 in 10, just a couple years ago.

Not because people got careless. Because an AI chat feels private. Like typing to a helpful friend, not submitting a form to a company's servers.

Every "things you should never tell ChatGPT" article says the same stuff: no Social Security numbers, no passwords, no bank details. True, and useless, because not one of those articles actually looks at the message you are about to send.

So I built something that does. Paste your message, it checks it for the risky stuff, tells you why in plain words, and shows you a cleaned version to send instead.

The part that matters most: it never leaves your browser. A privacy tool that uploads your text to check it would be lying about the one thing it promises.

Built it as AI Privacy Paste Checker. Post 2 covers the part I found genuinely interesting to build: making sure a "cleaned" message is actually clean.
